Overnight vs Intraday

Equal-weight basket of 50 large US stocks, rebalanced daily, 2005-01-04 to 2026-11-20 (5,506 trading days). Friction 11 bps per round trip.

Verdict

From 2005 to 2026, the equal-weight basket's return came mostly overnight (close to next open). Overnight-only compounded at +10.0% a year before costs, intraday-only at +5.8%, and buy & hold at +16.3% (the overnight leg accounts for 63% of the log return).

After 11 bps per round trip (paid every trading day, about 28% a year), overnight-only returns -16.6% a year and intraday-only -19.8%. Break-even is about 4.0 bps per round trip overnight and 2.7 bps intraday (where the net mean, Sharpe and t-stat hit zero; net CAGR turns negative a little earlier, at 3.8 and 2.2 bps).

Overnight-only net of costs does not survive: it needs a positive mean with t ≥ 2 in both halves, and got t = -5.5 and -4.8.

Intraday-only net of costs does not survive: it needs a positive mean with t ≥ 2 in both halves, and got t = -4.1 and -5.6.

Bottom line: the overnight effect is real as a description of where returns arrive, but neither leg is a tradable edge at this cost. Simply holding through both legs did better than trading either one.

Caveat: the basket is today's large caps, picked with hindsight (survivorship bias), so every absolute return here is flattered. The overnight vs intraday split is less exposed to that.

Survivorship bias and other caveats

  • Survivorship bias. The basket is today's list of large, successful companies. In 2005 this basket did not exist in this form: some names were small, some were not listed (they enter when their data starts), and companies that shrank, were acquired or went bankrupt are absent. Absolute returns are therefore biased upward. The split between overnight and intraday is less affected, but not immune.
  • Execution. Fills at the official open and close are assumed. Real auction fills, slippage on gaps and borrow/financing for an overnight position are folded into the single friction number.
  • Dividends are credited to the overnight leg on the ex-date. Withholding taxes are ignored.
  • Splits. Yahoo already split-adjusts its "raw" prices; each split is checked against the observed gap so prices are adjusted exactly once (see the data quality log).
  • Risk-free rate is taken as zero in the Sharpe ratio; the overnight strategy is in cash during the day and vice versa, which would earn a little interest in practice.
